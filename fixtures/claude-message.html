Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)

The SDGs are a set of 17 global goals created by the United Nations to make the world a better place by 2030. Think of them as a shared to-do list for countries, businesses, and people worldwide.

What are the 17 Goals?

  1. No Poverty — End poverty in all forms
  2. Zero Hunger — End hunger and improve nutrition
  3. Good Health — Ensure healthy lives for all
  4. Quality Education — Provide inclusive education
  5. Gender Equality — Achieve equality for women and girls
  6. Clean Water — Access to safe water and sanitation
  7. Affordable Energy — Clean and renewable energy for all
  8. Decent Work — Good jobs and economic growth
  9. Innovation & Infrastructure — Build resilient infrastructure
  10. Reduced Inequality — Reduce inequality within and between countries
  11. Sustainable Cities — Make cities safe and sustainable
  12. Responsible Consumption — Use resources responsibly
  13. Climate Action — Take action on climate change
  14. Life Below Water — Protect ocean life
  15. Life on Land — Protect forests and wildlife
  16. Peace & Justice — Peace, justice, and strong institutions
  17. Partnerships — Work together to achieve these goals

Why do they matter?

These goals tackle the biggest problems facing humanity: poverty, hunger, climate change, inequality, and lack of education. They recognize that fixing one problem often helps fix others.

Who's responsible?

Everyone — governments make policies, businesses change their practices, and individuals make choices (like buying sustainable products or supporting charities).
